## §1 Looking at the cards set aside on a card

**Request:** *"I want to be able to view cards that are held aside, like
with the Shilmulo Tarot card, as long as it says I can look at them at
any time. How this should work is by clicking on the card … and the
option that pops up should be to view the 2 set aside cards. And it
should have the teal tokens on it to represent how many cards are set
aside with it."*

A card in play can hold cards **out of play** on it — neither hand, nor
library, nor in play (`PermanentInPlay.stored`,
`docs/library-search-design.md` §5). The table drew none of it: Shilmulo
Tarot appeared as a bare equipment, and its two cards were only ever
visible for the instant the engine offered to draw one of them.

**The count, in teal.** `storeOverlay` is `counterOverlay`'s twin — the
same pips, the same colour, because what sits on a card and is not blood
reads teal at this table. Bottom **right**, since a card can carry
counters as well and those hold the bottom left. It is drawn from
`p.stored.length` and is therefore public: everybody can see that two
cards are on that equipment, which is true at a physical table too.

**The look is a menu entry, not a new gesture.** Clicking a card on the
table already opens a menu of what that card can do. "Look at the N cards
set aside on this" is one of the things this card can do, so it goes in
that menu rather than getting a click of its own — one gesture cannot
mean two things on the same element (the rule `wireTable` was written
under). It carries `data-peek` and deliberately **no `data-opt`**: the
single handler that submits an option id reads `data-opt`, so an entry
without one cannot be mistaken for a move. `tableActionMarks` counts it
in the badge, so a card with nothing else to do is still lit.

### The permission is not re-decided here

Six cards hold a store face down, and **every one of them lets its owner
look**: five print it — Shilmulo Tarot, Gift of Proteus, Storage Annex,
Delivery Truck, The Erciyes Fragments — and Maabara's card is public
anyway, since *"all other players can see which card is selected from the
ash heap"* [ANK 20200523].

So `readableStores` asks no question of its own. `maskStore` in
`redactFor` has already answered it: a store this viewer may read arrives
with its names on, one they may not arrives with `FACE_DOWN` names. The
UI reads the names back out and that **is** the same answer. A second
rule here would be a second chance to leak — the `redactFor` doc comment's
standing warning about "a second implementation of the masking rules".

`storePanel` re-checks against `readableStores` rather than trusting the
click, so a hand-set `storeOpen` or a stale click draws nothing.

**Where it is dead.** The whole table goes dark while an AI's move is
paced or a remote seat is deciding (`ctx` is null). The look goes with
it, which costs a second or two of a bot's delay and keeps one rule
about when the table is live rather than two.

### One drift removed on the way

`wireTable` used to re-derive `actionsByTableCard` and ask whether a
tile's option list was empty — a second copy of a question
`tableActionMarks` had already answered, and it stopped agreeing with it
the moment a card could be lit for something that is not an option. It
now reads the class the renderer put on the tile. **One question, one
place.**

## §2 Shilmulo Tarot moved a card every time it was asked

**Report:** *"Shilmulo Tarot seems bugged, it's allowing me to put any
cards from the top of my library onto it during my unlock phase.
Shouldn't it only allow me to do that once?"*

Yes. *"During your unlock phase, you can move **the top card** of your
library to this equipment"* — one card, once.

**Why it repeated.** The unlock window is not a single decision. `settle`
re-offers it until it has nothing left in it, so an unlock-phase ability
with no latch is offered again the moment it is used. The store's
`abilityOptions` in `compile.ts` gated `addTopInUnlockPhase` on the
window, the turn seat and a non-empty library, and on nothing else — so
a player could shovel their whole library onto it in one phase.

**The fix** is the latch the engine already has:
`PermanentInPlay.usedThisTurn`, cleared for every entry on `TurnBegan`,
which is what the archetypes' "once each turn" rides on. A seat's unlock
phase happens once a turn, so per turn and per unlock phase are the same
latch here. It is set in `useAbility` rather than at enumeration: the
latch means "the card has been moved", and only that path moves it.

`addTopInUnlockPhase` is set by exactly one card, so nothing else changed
behaviour.

**What this is an instance of.** *An early return meaning "nothing to do"
stops being true the day the effect gains a term* has a sibling: **an
option enumerator in a REPEATING window is offered again unless it says
otherwise.** Every other clause in that same `abilityOptions` is
self-limiting — `addFromHandInMasterPhase` consumes the hand card,
`addFromAshHeap` locks the card, `exchangeWithHandInMasterPhase` swaps —
and `addTopInUnlockPhase` was the one that consumed nothing observable
from the enumerator's point of view. Note also that the unlock phase now
**ends** immediately after the move on a board with no other unlock
ability, because the offer was the only thing in that window.

**Rulings checked.** *"There is no limit to the number of cards it can
store"* [PIB 20110712-2] is about the store's capacity, not about how
many times the clause fires; the fix leaves it uncapped.

Tests: `tests/cards/library-search.test.ts` — the offer is gone after one
use, and it is back after `TurnBegan`.

## §3 The terms on the vote bar

**Request:** *"During a referendum and players are voting, the terms of
said referendum should be summarised at the top in the vote bar next to
the name of the card. For instance: if someone plays Kine Resources
Contested, then it should show how the player allocated the points."*

The bar (`docs/table-ux-2026-09-18.md` §4) named the card, the caller,
the step and the running tally — everything except **what is being voted
on**. Terms are announced publicly before the vote (p. 27), so a player
had to scroll the log back to the announcement to find them.

**Why the params bag is not enough.** `ReferendumFrame.terms` is
`Record<string, string>`: `alloc` → `"Bob=3,Carol=1"`. That is not a
sentence, and worse, **a generic parse of it gets the sign backwards
between cards** — the same `alloc` key names the seats that LOSE on Kine
Resources Contested and the seats that GAIN on Parity Shift. That is
recorded in `docs/ai-referendum-view-design.md` §5.1, where the same
trap cost a cut feature.

**Two readings, in order:**

1. **The pool, by seat**, when the card declared a `seatMap` — through
   `resolvePerSeat`, the engine's own helper, which the terms decision
   and the AI's vote scorer already use. Signed, so a burn reads `Bob −3`
   and a gain reads `Carol +1`, coloured like the tally. A seat allocated
   nothing is left out: a zero beside two real numbers reads as a third
   term.
2. **The caller's own sentence** otherwise — a clan, a minion, a title, a
   location: terms that name no Methuselah.

**One new field, `ReferendumFrame.termsLabel`.** The engine wrote a
sentence for every terms option when it offered them ("Allocate:
Bob=3,Carol=1"); `chooseTerms` now keeps the one that was chosen. The
alternative was for the renderer to reconstruct it, which is the second
place that would have to know how thirteen construction sites spell their
params. One question, one place — the same reasoning that put
`perSeat` on the option in the first place.

Nothing leaks: this is the announcement, written down where everyone is
already looking. Absent before the terms step is answered, and absent for
a blood hunt, which has no terms (p. 35).

Tests: `tests/ui/vote-terms.test.ts`.

## §4 A vampire in torpor was being offered rescues and diableries

**Report:** *"A vampire in torpor can't rescue itself or anyone or commit
diablerie at all. Need to remove those options from vampires in torpor …
Make sure vampires in torpor perform like they should according to the
rules."*

**The engine had this right.** Probing the option list directly: a torpid
vampire is offered `leave:<id>` and nothing else. `canAct` starts at
`isReady`, so a torpid minion is never in `actors`; `canReact` does the
same for blocking and for reaction cards; and the diablerie/rescue
enumerator loops over `actors`, so a torpid vampire is never the actor.

**The table was wrong.** `actionsByTableCard` indexed a `takeAction`
under `o.minion` *and* under `o.targetMinion`, on the theory that the
victim's card is where a player looks for a rescue. What that actually
drew was a torpid vampire lit up with an eight-option menu — four
rescues and a diablerie per ready vampire on the board — every one of
which it may not perform. "A vampire in torpor can perform no action
except the leave torpor action" (p. 34), and the screen said the
opposite.

The victim indexing is gone. Both actions live on the **acting** vampire's
card, which is whose action it is, and the torpid vampire's card carries
its one legal action. The cost is a small one in discoverability, and it
is the right trade: a menu that offers a card actions it cannot take is
worse than one that makes you click the vampire that would act.

**This is a shape worth remembering.** Every one of the four rules about
torpor is enforced by a predicate that returns *false*, so every test of
them is a negative — and a negative passes just as happily when the
fixture never produced the option in the first place. That is why
`tests/cards/torpor-restrictions.test.ts` pairs each one with a ready
vampire beside the torpid one, with the same blood and the same
Disciplines, that IS offered the thing. Four assertions:

- no action but leave torpor, with `diablerize:V2:T1` and `rescue:V2:T1:*`
  live on the ready vampire;
- no rescue or diablerie **by** it, with a second torpid vampire present
  so "no target" cannot be the reason;
- no block and no reaction card, with the same Dominate on a ready
  vampire that is offered Deflection;
- **and the positive the rule actually grants:** it *can* play an action
  modifier during its own leave-torpor action (the blocker needs real
  intercept, or p. 26's "only when needed" hides the stealth card for a
  reason that has nothing to do with torpor).

## §5 The one that came next, in its own doc

**Garibaldi-Meucci Museum's exchange menu** (owner report, 2026-09-21) is
the same family of complaint as §3: a decision whose options are correct
and unreadable. A hand × ash-heap pair list became one activation plus two
card pickers. Written up where the card lives —
`cheap-tail-design.md` §1 — because the reasoning it overturns is written
there, and a future reader of the mechanic is the one who needs it. The
generalisable part: **a cross-product menu can usually become sequential
pickers for free, and the sequencing is unobservable when the steps are
choice frames** (no impulse cycle, nothing else moves in between).
