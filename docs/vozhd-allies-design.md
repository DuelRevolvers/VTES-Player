# The Vozhd, and an ally's own weapons

*The Vozhd of Sofia (102267), The Vozhd of Gravesend (102363), The Vozhd
of Juiz de Fora (102364), The Vozhd of Szczecin (102365), City Star Taxi
(102234).*

**Ally is the largest unsupported family (14) and has never had a wave of
its own**, even though the allies/retainers kernel has been built since
`docs/allies-retainers-design.md`. Seven of the fourteen are BLOCKED
(wraith or zombie, pending owner review); of the seven buildable, **four
are the Vozhd and they are one card printed four times** with a different
last clause each. City Star Taxi joins them because it shares the
mechanism the Vozhd of Sofia needs and nothing else in the pool has: an
ally with a printed strike of its own.

Left for the follow-up wave: **Vagabond Mystic** (102087) and
**Underbridge Stray** (102065), the two buildable allies that are not
part of either mechanism.

## 1. The shared skeleton

```
Unique ghoul with 5 life. N strength, 0 bleed.
After this ally enters play, burn an ally or retainer you control.
This ally can enter combat with a <minion|vampire> as a Ⓓ action.
<one or two clauses that differ>
```

The middle two lines already exist: `permanent.rushGrant`'s ally form
(`spec.rush`) and — for the enter-play burn — **War Ghoul**, which prints
the identical sentence and hand-rolled it, putting the victim's id in the
play option (`play:War Ghoul:basic:<actor>:<burn>:<card>`).

**`ally.enterPlayBurn` generalises that**, because four more cards print
it. War Ghoul itself is deliberately **not** rewritten onto it — it is
supported and green, and rewriting a working card is a separate checkable
change (the Platinum Protocol precedent) — but it is recorded in
`partial-support.md` as a retrofit, and the ledger row about *how* it
offers the burn is answered below.

**Reading on record, and it settles War Ghoul's owner-flagged row: the
entering ally IS a legal victim.** "Burn an ally or retainer you control"
names the set, and at the moment the clause fires the new ally is one of
them; nothing in the sentence excludes it or requires an alternative to
be preferred. So `enterPlayBurn` offers `self` alongside the others, at
every board state, exactly as War Ghoul does. Recorded rather than
guessed at a second time.

## 2. `ally.strike` — the ally's own attack

"It can strike: 3R damage" (Sofia) and "This ally can strike: 1R damage"
(City Star Taxi) are the first **printed strikes belonging to a minion
rather than to a weapon**. The machinery is already there: `spec.weapon`
compiles to an `abilityOptions` in `combat.chooseStrike` that calls
`ops.chooseWeaponStrike`, and an ally's card text lives as a
**self-attached entry**, which is precisely where that ability looks.

So `ally.strike` reuses it with **one difference that matters**: the
weapon path returns nothing when `cf.restrict[side].equipment` is set
("the opposing minion cannot use equipment", Terror Frenzy). **An ally's
own body is not equipment**, so the restriction must not reach it. That
one line is the whole reason this is a separate clause rather than
`weapon: {...}` on an ally spec — and writing it as a weapon would also
have made every equipment-burner (Disarm, `actionOnPermanent`) able to
destroy the ally's arms.

City Star Taxi's "1 optional maneuver each combat" needs nothing new:
`PermanentStatics.maneuverPerCombat` was built in the play-from-hand wave
and is granted at `pushCombat`.

## 3. "Plays cards as a vampire" — THE DEFERRED GATE, and the rulebook
   defines it completely

`CLAUDE.md` has carried "'plays cards as a vampire' allies deferred
(Spectral Servitor, Vozhd of Juiz de Fora)" as a known deviation since
the allies/retainers wave. The rulebook specifies it in full (p. 11,
Advanced Rules — Allies), and every clause turns out to be something this
engine either already does or gets for free:

> "Allies are minions other than vampires that you as a Methuselah can
> control. Some allies have the ability to play certain cards **'as a
> vampire.'** In these cases, **the ally is treated as a vampire for all
> effects generated by the play of the card**, including duration effects
> (like 'for the remainder of combat'). **The ally's life represents
> their blood (to pay costs etc.).** Any blood they gain or lose as a
> vampire equates to a gain or loss of life for the ally. **For purposes
> of that card, the ally has a capacity of 1** for use if the card
> requires an older vampire or a vampire of a given capacity. **If the
> ally gains life in excess of their capacity, it does not drain off**,
> and if the effect inflicts aggravated damage on the ally, they burn
> life as normal. However, **if the effect would send the ally to torpor,
> then they are burned instead.** The ally is treated as a vampire **only
> for the effect generated from playing the card**. In particular, the
> ally is **not** treated as a vampire for effects the card has from
> being in play (like 'the vampire with this card' effects)."

| The rule | Where it already lives |
|---|---|
| life *is* blood, for costs and gains | `MinionState.blood` with the `kind` discriminant — the allies/retainers wave's central choice |
| excess life does not drain off | `drainOverCapacity()` skips `kind !== "vampire"`, and the `BloodGained` clamp does too — both already cite p. 11 |
| aggravated burns life as normal | the damage pipeline's ally branch |
| torpor becomes a burn | `sendToTorpor`'s ally branch, the `strikeIncapacitate` reading |
| not a vampire for in-play effects | nothing grants an ally in-play statics from this |

**What was actually missing was one field.** The combat and modifier
compilers pick their player with `disciplineOk(m, mode)`, which reads
`disciplinesOf(m)` — they never ask `m.kind`. So an ally whose
`MinionState.disciplines` contains `{ ani: "basic" }` is offered exactly
the cards the clause names, through the ordinary path, with no new gate
in the enumerators. `ally.playsAsVampire` puts it there at
`AllyEnteredPlay`.

**Recorded gap, deliberately not built: the DEFAULT capacity of 1.** The
rulebook's default matters only for a card that asks about the player's
capacity, and Juiz de Fora overrides it anyway ("as a vampire with
capacity **5**") — which happens to equal its starting life, the number
`capacityOf` already returns for an ally. So the two answers coincide for
the only buildable card in the family, and a separate `playAsCapacity`
would be a field with no reader. It is written down here so the next card
that needs it does not have to re-derive that this is why.

## 4. The four last clauses

| Card | Clause | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Sofia** | "During your unlock phase, you can discard an ally or retainer card to add 2 life to this ally." | `onControllerUnlock` + `CardHandler.costTypes` (the query built for the play-cost wave) to filter the hand; the discard is **replaced** (p. 7, and the toll wave's correction) |
| **Gravesend** | "This ally can prevent 1 damage each round of combat." | `preventDamageAbility(..., "round")` — War Ghoul's, as data |
| **Gravesend** | "During combat, this ally can burn 1 life to cancel a strike card as it is played by the opposing minion, and its cost is not paid (the minion chooses a strike again)." | §5 |
| **Juiz de Fora** | "During your unlock phase, this ally can steal any amount of blood (becoming life) from a Tzimisce you control, not to exceed its starting life." | the `x=N` shape (one option per affordable amount), capped by `capacityOf` |
| **Szczecin** | "Once each combat, this ally can discard a card requiring Protean [pro] to prevent 2 damage." | `CombatFrame.usedThisCombat` (Guardian Angel) + `CardHandler.requiresDisciplines` (the discipline-filtered wave) reading the **hand** |
| **Szczecin** | "During any other Methuselah's minion phase, a Tzimisce you control can burn 1 blood to unlock this ally." | an `abilityOptions` gated on `ctx.turnSeat !== owner.seat` — the mirror of the "during YOUR phase" gate the 2026-08-02 bug installed |

**Four of the six are existing machinery reached from a new place**,
which is the fifth wave running where a query built for one family turned
the next into data. The two that are not are Gravesend's cancel and the
`x=N` steal.

## 5. Cancelling a strike card, by an ally, with no card of its own

Gravesend's second clause is the only genuinely new sequencing question
in the wave, and it decomposes into three pieces that all exist:

1. **Acting in `card.asPlayed`.** `abilityOptionsFor` returns `[]` there
   — "p. 7 allows only cancels and wakes in another card's as-played
   period" — so a card in play opts in with
   **`CardHandler.abilityInAsPlayed`**, built for Meditative Grove.
2. **Knowing the pending card is a STRIKE card.** A card never reads
   another card's spec; the answer is denormalized onto the frame when it
   is pushed, beside `isMaster` / `isCombat` / `isReaction` / `isFrenzy`.
   **`CardPlayFrame.isStrike`** is the new one, from the central query
   **`CardHandler.isStrikeCard`**, which `compileSpec` answers for free
   from `combatWindowFor(mode) === "combat.chooseStrike"` — the same test
   `costTypes` already uses to label a mode `"strike"`.
3. **Cancelling.** `ops.cancelPendingCard(refundCost)` already exists.
   **`refundCost: true`**, because "its cost is not paid" is the Sudden
   Reversal wording and Sudden Reversal passes `true`. Its doc comment
   notes it is normally called while the cancelling *card* resolves, with
   the cancelled play on top; an **ability** has no frame of its own, so
   the cancelled play is on top there too, and the call is the same.

**"The minion chooses a strike again" needs no code at all.** A cancelled
strike card never sets `cf.strikes[side]`, and `chooseStrike` is a step
the settle loop returns to while that slot is null. The parenthetical is
describing the engine's existing behaviour, not asking for new behaviour
— which is worth stating, because reading it as a new instruction is how
a re-entrancy bug gets written.

**It is "the OPPOSING minion", not either.** Gravesend must be a
combatant and the pending card's player must be the other side. A card
played by a bystander (the outside-the-combat gate) is not the opposing
minion's and is not cancellable by this.

## 6. What this wave does not do

- **War Ghoul is not rewritten** onto `ally.enterPlayBurn` (§1), and
  **Spectral Servitor stays BLOCKED** — it is a wraith, and its
  "plays cards as a vampire" half being available now does not unblock
  the rest of it.
- **Vagabond Mystic and Underbridge Stray** are the buildable remainder
  of the family and are a follow-up, not a cut: Vagabond Mystic wants
  "cannot block vampires" (a filtered `cannotBlock`) and Underbridge
  Stray wants a burn-this-ally reaction during a directed action. Both
  are recorded in `partial-support.md` so the next survey does not
  mistake them for blocked.
