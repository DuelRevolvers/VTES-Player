# Ten owner reports from a play session — v0.10.6

All from one sitting at the real build. Grouped by what they turned out to
be, because three of them were the same bug wearing different clothes.

## 1. The magnifier kept vanishing

> "Card previews disappear with every new little action, it needs to stay
> persistent as long as the cursor is hovering over it."

Every repaint is `innerHTML =`. That destroys the element the pointer was
over, and **the browser fires no fresh `mouseover` for a replacement that
appears under a stationary cursor** — so the preview went away on every
tick of the game and only came back when the mouse moved. `DebugApp` now
remembers what is under the pointer and where the pointer is, and
`paint()` puts the preview back.

It re-asks the DOM what is under the pointer *now* rather than trusting
the remembered card: a repaint may have moved the table (a card burned, a
minion to torpor), and showing a preview of a card that is no longer there
is worse than showing none. It also does **not** restart the name's fade —
the card has not changed, so re-announcing it every repaint would be the
same flicker in another form.

> "After I move a card in my hand, the card previews don't show up until I
> click somewhere else."

Different mechanism, same symptom. `holding` suppresses the preview while
a card is being dragged, and it was cleared on `dragend`/`drop` — but
after a hand reorder the dragged element **has already been re-rendered**,
so `dragend` fires on a node that is no longer in the tree and never
reaches the delegated listener. `holding` stayed true until the next
unrelated click. The fix does not chase the event: any `mousemove` with no
button down means nothing is held, whatever events did or did not arrive.

> "I want to be able to hover over the names of cards in the game log and
> it show the card preview."

The magnifier keyed on `img[data-zoom]`. It now keys on **any**
`[data-zoom]`, and log lines carry card names as
`<span class="cardref" data-zoom=... data-name=...>`. The name comes from
`data-name` for a text reference and from `alt` for a scan, so one handler
serves the table, the log and the deck lists.

Names are found with a single regex built from the registry, **longest
first** — ordered alternation means "Praxis Seizure: Berlin" has to be
offered before "Berlin", or the shorter name cuts the longer one in half.
Escaping happens before matching: card names contain apostrophes and
accents but no HTML, so no tag can be forged by one.

## 2. Action menus were painting UNDER other cards

> "The card actions for a card in Torpor were being covered by cards in my
> uncontrolled area."

`.playmenu` already had `z-index: 20`, and that was never going to be
enough: **z-index only orders siblings within a stacking context**, and
each mat, zone and card row is one. The torpor card's menu was ordered
correctly inside the torpor zone, and then the whole uncontrolled zone — a
later sibling — painted over it.

The fix lifts the *ancestors* rather than the menu:
`:has(.actionable.selected)` raises every container that holds the open
card, for as long as it holds it. That is a CSS answer to a CSS problem;
doing it in the renderer would have meant threading "is something selected
inside me" through four levels of markup.

## 3. Seats were in deal order, not table order

> "Change the order of prey and predator to be around the table, rather
> than in a line."

The seat array *is* table order (your prey is the next seat), but the mats
were drawn from index 0, so your prey and predator landed wherever the
deal put them and you had to read each mat's "prey / predator" line to
work out which way round the table went. `seatsAroundTable` rotates the
array so **you are always first and the direction of play is the direction
you read in**. A spectator has no seat and gets the array as dealt.

## 4. You can now look through your own decks

> "Add the ability to view your Library and Crypt decks by clicking on
> them, but it needs to be alphabetically, so it doesn't show the current
> deck order."

The owner's own condition is the rules argument, and it is exactly right.
p. 16 keeps a library face down **even to its owner**, so a list in deck
order would hand you your next draws. What a player legitimately knows is
what they built: *composition, never order* — the same line the AI is held
to. Sorting alphabetically destroys the order and leaves precisely that.

Only your own piles are drawn as buttons, and the list is by name with
counts, because "how many Villeins are left" is the question being asked.

## 5. The log's commonest line said the least

> "Sometimes when a player does an action with one of their vampires, the
> game will just say that vampire takes an action. It needs to say what
> that is."

`ActionKind` has five named kinds and `cardEffect`, which is every action
a card announces — so the most frequent line in the game read "V1 takes an
action" and never said which card. `ActionAnnounced` now carries
`cardName`, set at both announce sites (a card play knows its own card; a
granted action knows its entry), and the log reads "takes the Govern the
Unaligned action".

## 6. The AI was giving cards away

> "The AI put a good master card (Vessel) onto its prey. The AI should not
> put a card onto a vampire they don't control unless it helps out the AI
> that played it."

Correct, and the enumeration was right: Vessel says "a vampire" and the
rules mean any. It is the *choice* that was wrong.

The guard is written in terms of what the play **does**, not which card it
is — the AI does not read card text, by design (that would be a second,
drifting model of the pool). A play that targets a minion this seat does
not control must carry at least one hostile effect (`deny`, `damage`,
`poolDrain`, `steal`) or it is not considered. A corruption counter on an
opponent's vampire still goes through; a blood engine does not.

## 7. Joining a game already in progress

> "Add the ability for people to join mid-game. If spots are full, then
> they become a spectator. If they accidentally backed out of the game,
> then they should be able to jump back into their spot by re-joining, as
> long as they weren't kicked by the host. It should only be spots that
> were once player controlled."

A mid-game arrival used to be hung up on — which made an accidental
back-button **permanent**, since the seat had already gone to a bot.

`LobbyHost.onLateJoin` now has exactly two outcomes:

- **their old seat back**, when the name they arrive under matches a seat,
  nobody is holding it, and the host did not remove them;
- **a spectator's view otherwise** — table full, new name, seat was always
  a bot's, or they were kicked.

The client needs no new message type: it is sent the lobby snapshot (which
carries `you`, and so says whether it is sitting down or watching) and
then `started` — the same pair a player who was there from the beginning
gets.

Three conditions, each answered in the one place that can answer it:

- **Was this seat ever a person's?** `HostSession` is constructed with the
  seats that were `remote` at the deal. A seat that was a bot from the
  start is part of the game the others agreed to, and a stranger taking it
  over changes the table they sat down at.
- **Were they kicked?** `kick()` now records the seat. A kick is not a
  disconnection and the door does not reopen.
- **Is the seat free?** Only the session knows who is still connected, so
  `seatIsRejoinable` is asked there rather than re-derived in the lobby.

**And the seat comes back off the bot.** `takeOver` hands a departed seat
to a `HeuristicAgent`, and that agent keeps answering — so without
clearing it a returning player would sit down and watch a bot play their
turns. `onHello` drops the agent and the "Bot" label together. An empty
label now *deletes* the entry rather than storing `""`: the mats test for
the key, not its value, so a seat taken back would otherwise still be
drawn as a bot's.

## What is tested

Two lobby tests, replacing the one that pinned the old refusal: a stranger
arriving mid-game is **not** hung up on and holds no seat, and a player who
dropped out gets their own seat back by name. The rest is rendering and
hover behaviour — the existing render tests cover structure, and only the
real build shows the feel.

# Follow-up — v0.10.7

Two of the ten were wrong on the real table.

## The deck viewer showed one row of nothing

It read `seat.library`, and `redactFor` masks both piles to face-down
cards **for everyone including their owner** — which is p. 14 and is
right. So the panel had nothing to list.

The composition therefore has to come from the redaction itself:
`SeatState.ownPiles` is added for the viewer's own seat, as **sorted
names**. Sorting there rather than in the UI is the point — for a guest
the view crosses the wire, so sorting in the renderer would ship the real
draw order and merely decline to draw it. Sorted in `redactFor`, the
order never leaves the host. `deckList` is what they brought; this is
what is left.

**Two hidden-information tests went red, and were right to look.** Both
collect every OTHER seat's library names and assert none appear anywhere
in the view. Two names did — and both were in the viewer's own deck as
well, because precons share cards. A name in two decks cannot tell a leak
from the viewer's own copy, so it can support neither verdict; the tests
now subtract the viewer's own library first. A name only the others hold
still fires, which is the whole of what they are for.

## The mats were in a line, not a ring

> "Messy (me) … the player on my right, which is Bot 2, would be my
> predator, which they are not. This needs to go around the table
> clockwise."

Exactly right. The rotation from v0.10.6 put the seats in table order —
me, prey, prey's prey, predator — and then the CSS grid laid them out
**row-major**, left-to-right on both rows:

    Messy   Bot 1 (prey)
    Bot 2   Bot 3 (predator)

which reads along the top row and then jumps back to the left, so the
predator ends up diagonally opposite and the seat below you is your
prey's prey.

`seatsAroundTable` now walks the grid's **perimeter clockwise** — across
the top, down the right, back along the bottom, up the left — and places
the rotated seats into those cells, returning the grid row-major for the
CSS to fill:

    Messy   Bot 1 (prey)
    Bot 3   Bot 2
    (predator)

**Holes are kept as nulls** (a `.matgap`) rather than filtered out: with
an odd seat count, dropping the empty cell would let every later mat slide
up into the gap and undo the placement. Verified for 2–5 seats.
